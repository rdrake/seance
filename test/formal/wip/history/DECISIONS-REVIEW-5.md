**Verdict: not yet as specified.** Delivery's finite bounds are sufficient, but D15 leaves response handling ambiguous and the new properties do not catch all the mutants attributed to them. Fix the batch-error/closure rules and confirmation conditions before writing `Delivery.tla` (DECISIONS.md:267–292; DESIGN.md:145–161).

Reviewed both v3 diffs and the permitted local texts; D15 is treated as a stated policy, without inspecting client code. Spec prefixes below denote the paths in RULES.md:13–22; the supplied source commit was not independently verified.

1. **D15: mostly valid client policy, not a complete protocol outcome table.** Labeled echoes may confirm even when their text was modified, and even when the server faked an echo for a blocked message; confirmation therefore cannot mean delivery to the target (DECISIONS.md:269; EM:21–24, 50–52). Error, disconnect and timeout outcomes are defensible local classifications, but disconnect does not prove “not sent.” `ACK` means a command normally produces no response, not that a chat message was successfully delivered. With echo-message enabled, an ordinary PRIVMSG requires an echo; ACK-only is already explicitly an adversarial `Lossy` outcome, not an alternative conforming success response (DECISIONS.md:24, 64–69, 270, 275–276; LR:68, 76–83; EM:21–24).

   **Missing/overlapping case:** an error inside a labeled batch has `batch=`, not its own label. Does it fail the copy, immediately or at closure under `Deferred`? The table's “labeled 4xx or FAIL” does not say, while “closes with no echo” says it remains pending even if it contained an error. Specify inherited label attribution and make the latter row mean *no settling response*, with explicit precedence. Multi-line responses must be batched and empty batches are allowed (DECISIONS.md:78–90, 271–272; LR:55–58; BA:51, 54–58, 63). Likewise, an unlabeled modified echo that fails exact-text matching is ordinary incoming data and leaves the original pending; make clear that the no-match row includes that case (DECISIONS.md:273–274; EM:22–24).

   The self-message paragraph is conditional on there being no other matching pending copy. After the labeled echo settles A, its unlabeled delivered copy can settle another pending identical self-message B under the stated oldest-match rule. It is not automatically ignored as an acknowledgment, and displaying both copies is not LR's suggested deduplication method 2, though that advice is non-normative (DECISIONS.md:273, 281–286; LR:74, 87–92).

2. **The added checks help, but their mutant claims overreach.** Disabling timeout for `ResolvedByResponse` removes timeout-only success; `ConfirmedByOwn` independently checks provenance. Keep fair sending/delivery and processing explicit in that configuration (DESIGN.md:145–154; DECISIONS.md:234–237). However, allowing any confirmed/accepted/failed terminal result does not verify the response-to-outcome table: a client that fails every request, or a connection close before processing, can satisfy it. To expose ignored echoes, cover an echo-only, connection-open run with no alternate settlement; check outcome correspondence separately (DESIGN.md:145–157; DECISIONS.md:269–276).

   Wrong-label confirmation is caught when distinct request provenance is recorded and an affected copy is actually confirmed. The terminal-change mutant is caught by the absorbing check (DESIGN.md:148–161; DECISIONS.md:238–242). **Early deferred processing is not caught by `ConfirmedByOwn`:** confirming A at A's inner echo satisfies the stated provenance rule even before closure. Truncating other batch content does not change that fact; a partial batch with no echo cannot exercise early echo confirmation. Add a separate condition that settlement under `Deferred` waits for the enclosing response's outermost closure, with a cover for echo received while that batch remains open. This is client policy, not a protocol MUST (DESIGN.md:149, 159–160; DECISIONS.md:88–91; BA:55, 63).

3. **Other-session witness: reachable, but the claimed sufficient condition is false.** One local request A to the channel, an indistinguishable unlabeled line from the other session, then A's own labeled echo suffices; fallback confirms A on the wrong line. This needs neither another local label nor two late replies, so it fits the three-request/one-late-response bounds. Enable fallback in the positive configuration and flip only other-session on, as the witness convention requires (DECISIONS.md:37–38, 288–292; DESIGN.md:51–55, 70–71). The concrete environment should be a shared-identity/bouncer relay: EM:44–48 describes it; LR:60 concerns delivery to one's own nick and does not require forwarding between arbitrary sessions merely because their account matches.

   **Other-session off is insufficient.** Send identical channel copies A and B; time A out; deliver A's late unlabeled echo while B is pending. Fallback confirms B using A's line. This uses two labels and one late reply, within the bounds, with no other session (DECISIONS.md:69, 273, 278–279; DESIGN.md:52–54). The self-copy collision above supplies another counterexample (DESIGN.md:152–154). Fallback off is sufficient under correct label attribution; fallback on needs explicit exclusion of *all* cross-copy matches, not just other sessions. Keep origin/request identity and echo-versus-delivered-copy identity in the ghosts, including at settlement, so the safety property can distinguish identical wire contents (DESIGN.md:66–69, 148–154). Do not weaken it to exact-text equality: that would hide precisely the hazard being tested.
